---

**\[Everyone thinks X.]**

Every startup founder to corrected to think: "What problem are we solving".

Startup founders are taught to find a problem and solve it.
  
**\[But Y.]**  

The world is full of products that work perfectly and nobody talks about.

Most successful products solve a problem.
In UX, we get into the psyche of the user to understand their problem
Algo's operate on a broader scale and just seent the trends without know or caring why,

Yet many products that solve real problems still fail.

- Better to-do apps fail.
- Better CRMs fail.
- Better note-taking apps fail.
- Better project management tools fail.

Why?

But solving problems doesn't lead to delight.
When did you last tell your friend about your recent trip to the toilet?

Problems solved, doesn't lead to success.

**\[Because Z.]** 
**\[Investigate]** 

Solving a problem gives people a reason to use something.

It doesn't give them a reason to talk about it.

Nobody tells their friends about a toilet.

Nobody posts screenshots of a filing cabinet.

Nobody excitedly shares the fact that their spreadsheet balances.

Interesting products spread.

Useful products don't necessarily spread.

Delighting leads to success.
Delighted users leads to word-of mouth.

Solving the problem is a hygiene-factor – table stakes.


  Examples:
- You arrive on a beautiful horse. I pull up in a motorcar
- You wait months for your product team to ship that app. I asked my buddy Claude.



**\[Which means the real issue is A.]**

Solving the problem is table stakes.

The real question is:

What makes somebody tell another person?

People don't talk about solutions.

They talk about surprises.
