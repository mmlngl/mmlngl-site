# Evaluation

## Subject

What “oversubscribed” really means in practice for product/service businesses.

## Observations

- “Oversubscribed” means having more demand than you can handle.
- To know if you’re oversubscribed, you first need to know your capacity.
- In agency work, capacity is constrained by team size.
- Campaigns exist to collect signals from the market.
- Signals vary in strength: **money > time > effort**.
- There’s often a smaller “signal product” people buy before becoming core customers.
- Showing waitlist size can increase perceived demand.
- Lead requirements vary by lead quality: hot < warm < cold.
- Pipeline stages can be formalised: Leads → Appointments → Presentations → Sales.
- Marketing spend can be increased in response to missing pipeline demand.
- The system works best when planned **bottom-up from capacity**, not top-down from budget.

The book keeps reframing growth as a system of **capacity, filtering, and flow control**.

## Act 2 — Investigation

### Section 1 — Start with capacity, not ambition
- The overlooked first question: what can you actually handle?
- In services, capacity is concrete:
  - people
  - project slots
  - onboarding bandwidth
  - management attention
- This flips the usual planning model:
  - not “we have £X to spend, what happens?”
  - but “we can serve Y clients, what must the pipeline look like?”

### Section 2 — Not all demand is equal
- Signals differ in strength:
  - **money > time > effort**
- A paid signal means more than a signup.
- A booked call means more than a like.
- A filled application means more than passive interest.
- This is where “signal products” matter:
  - small commitments before the main commitment
  - a way to qualify intent before heavy sales effort

### Section 3 — A pipeline is a queue, not a list
- Leads → Appointments → Presentations → Sales
- The pipeline is not admin hygiene.
- It is the mechanism that:
  - sequences demand
  - filters weak intent
  - protects capacity
  - turns interest into scheduled intake

### Section 4 — Marketing is downstream of capacity
- The usual model: set a budget, then accept whatever lead flow appears.
- The stronger model: derive required lead flow from delivery capacity.
- If pipeline input is too low, spend more.
- If fulfillment is constrained, don’t just pour more leads into the top.
- Marketing becomes **flow regulation**, not just promotion.

### Section 5 — Waitlists and visible demand
- Transparency can shape behavior.
- Showing a waiting list does not just prove interest.
- It can:
  - pace demand
  - increase urgency
  - make scarcity legible
- Waitlists are part of queue design.
