I'm reading [[Oversubscribed by Daniel Priestley]]. He makes the point:

> Markets go up with the number of buyers exceeds the number of sellers

or, $Q_b > Q_s$

Equipped with this new lens, I applied the idea to see what I could find.

For any market, $m$:
$$Q_b = k_m . Q_s$$
A market is "up" when $k_m > 1$.
Let's rearrange to make $k_m$ the subject.

$$
k_m = \frac{Q_b}{Q_s}
$$
where:
- $k_m$ = Market Coefficient
- $Q_b$ = Quantity of buyers
- $Q_s$ = Quantity of sellers

The Market Coefficient, $k_m$, is an indicator how "up" a market is.

This is also Supply vs Demand.

**Assumption**: what if the degree of "how up a market is" was the same as the degree of "success for an endeavour"?

Then, Success is when demand outstrips supply, or

$$
C = \frac{S}{D}
$$
I played this out.

John wants a life partner, how well does he do? What's his success?

There's only one John.
There are many potential partners.
Is John a "wanter"?
All potential partners can provide the role.
In any case, $S$ is low.

Dead-end. It needs a macroscopic view.

I reframed: John is the "provider" of love. He has love to sell. He's not alone.
Many people are potential partners. They will buy his love.

$$
S = \frac{{{john}} + {{john's\ competition}}}{{potential\ partners}}
$$

Or,

$$
S = \frac{1 + C_{{john}}}N
$$
