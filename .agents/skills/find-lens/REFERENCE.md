# Find Lens Reference

## Purpose

A lens is a compressed mental model that changes how people interpret a situation.

The purpose of this skill is not to evaluate whether something is correct.

The purpose is to evaluate whether a proposed lens:

- Explains observations
- Resolves tension
- Creates a new perspective
- Compresses complexity
- Survives after the article is forgotten

---

# Domain Terminology

## Observation

A concrete fact.

Examples:

- Startup A solved a problem and failed.
- Agents repeatedly ask the same questions.
- Plans are frequently recreated.

Observations are evidence.

Observations are not interpretations.

---

## Pattern

A recurring relationship between observations.

Examples:

- Information is repeatedly lost.
- Useful products often fail.
- Teams struggle to find existing knowledge.

Patterns emerge from multiple observations.

---

## Existing Belief

The assumption the audience currently holds.

Template:

```text
Everyone thinks X.
```

Examples:

- Startups succeed by solving problems.
- Buildings have a documentation problem.
- More AI training data is always better.

---

## Tension

A contradiction between expectation and reality.

Template:

```text
People expect X.

Yet Y happens.
```

Examples:

- We have more information than ever, yet nobody can find what they need.
- Useful products fail.
- AI has more data than ever, yet training quality may be declining.

No tension = no thought piece.

---

## Reclassification

Changing the category of the problem.

Examples:

| Before                | After                |
| --------------------- | -------------------- |
| Documentation problem | Memory problem       |
| Data problem          | Food supply problem  |
| Product problem       | Distribution problem |
| Sales problem         | Information problem  |

Most strong lenses involve category shifts.

---

## Lens

A compressed mental model.

Examples:

- Buildings have a memory problem.
- AI has a food supply problem.
- Sales capacity becomes limited by support capacity.
- People don't share solutions. They share stories.

A lens explains observations.

A lens is not advice.

---

## Reframe

The moment the audience sees the problem differently.

A successful reframe changes interpretation without changing facts.

---

# Supported Evaluation Lenses

## Explanatory Lens

Evaluates:

```text
How much does this lens explain?
```

Examples:

- Buildings have a memory problem.
- AI has a food supply problem.

Best for:

- Thought leadership
- Industry insights
- Strategy

---

## Category Shift Lens

Evaluates:

```text
Does this lens move the problem into a more useful category?
```

Examples:

- Documentation → Memory
- Product → Distribution
- AI → Data Ecology

---

## Memorability Lens

Evaluates:

```text
Will people repeat this?
```

Examples:

Strong:

- AI has a food supply problem.

Weak:

- Synthetic training datasets may introduce recursive quality degradation.

---

## Portability Lens

Evaluates:

```text
Can this lens be applied elsewhere?
```

Examples:

Strong:

- People don't share solutions. They share stories.

Weak:

- This startup's onboarding flow needs improvement.

---

## Thought Leadership Lens

Evaluates:

```text
Does this create a genuinely new perspective?
```

Strong:

- Buildings have a memory problem.

Weak:

- Documentation is important.

---

# Scoring Rubric

## 1–3

Weak.

Usually:

- Advice
- Observation
- Conclusion
- Slogan

Examples:

- Startups should focus on users.
- Documentation is important.

---

## 4–6

Interesting.

Contains a partial reframe.

Limited explanatory power.

Examples:

- Startups focus too much on problem solving.

---

## 7–8

Strong.

Explains multiple observations.

Creates a useful category shift.

Examples:

- Utility gets adoption. Remarkability gets distribution.

---

## 9–10

Exceptional.

Creates a durable mental model.

Explains many observations.

Portable beyond the original domain.

Examples:

- Buildings have a memory problem.
- Customers hire products to do jobs.
- AI has a food supply problem.

---

# Evidence Hierarchy

## Level 1 — Single Observation

Weakest.

```text
One thing happened.
```

Example:

- Startup A failed.

---

## Level 2 — Multiple Observations

Better.

```text
Several similar things happened.
```

Example:

- Startup A failed.
- Startup B failed.
- Startup C failed.

---

## Level 3 — Pattern

Strong.

```text
The same dynamic appears repeatedly.
```

Example:

- Useful products repeatedly fail.

---

## Level 4 — Cross-Domain Pattern

Very strong.

```text
The same dynamic appears in multiple industries.
```

Example:

- Information loss occurs in construction, healthcare, and government.

---

## Level 5 — Universal Pattern

Strongest.

```text
The lens explains behaviour across systems.
```

Example:

- People don't share solutions. They share stories.

---

# Decision Rules

## Keep The Lens If

- It explains multiple observations.
- It resolves tension.
- It creates a category shift.
- It can be repeated from memory.
- It generates new insights.

---

## Improve The Lens If

- It explains only one observation.
- It sounds clever but teaches nothing.
- It requires lengthy explanation.
- Better alternatives exist.

---

## Reject The Lens If

- It is advice.
- It is a conclusion.
- It is a prediction.
- It is merely a slogan.

---

# Anti-Patterns

## Advice Disguised As Lens

Bad:

```text
Startups should delight users.
```

Advice.

Not a lens.

---

## Conclusion Disguised As Lens

Bad:

```text
Documentation is important.
```

Conclusion.

Not a lens.

---

## Clever But Empty

Bad:

```text
Startup founders face a crisis of faith.
```

Memorable.

Poor explanatory power.

---

## Observation Disguised As Lens

Bad:

```text
AI models train on AI-generated content.
```

Fact.

Not a lens.

---

## Overfitted Lens

Bad:

A lens that only explains one example.

Strong lenses explain many examples.

---

# Edge Cases

## Multiple Good Lenses

When several lenses score similarly:

- Prefer explanatory power.
- Prefer memorability.
- Prefer portability.

---

## No Tension Exists

If no contradiction exists:

- Gather more observations.
- Search for hidden assumptions.
- Reframe the problem.

---

## Lens Is Better Than Evidence

Sometimes a lens sounds good but is unsupported.

Require stronger observations.

---

## Evidence Is Better Than Lens

Sometimes observations are strong but the lens is weak.

Generate more alternatives.

---

# Escalation Criteria

Generate additional candidate lenses if:

- Explanatory Power < 7
- Reframe Strength < 7
- Memorability < 7

Restart reclassification if:

- The lens feels like advice.
- The lens feels like a conclusion.
- The lens explains only one observation.

Search for analogies if:

- The category shift is weak.
- The lens feels obvious.
- The lens lacks novelty.

Continue generating alternatives until:

- One lens explains the observations.
- One lens resolves the tension.
- One lens feels inevitable after hearing it.

---

# Golden Rule

The article is not the product.

The lens is the product.

The article merely earns the right to install it.
