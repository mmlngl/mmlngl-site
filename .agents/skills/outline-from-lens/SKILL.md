# Write From Lens

## Quick start

A lens is the product.

The article is the delivery mechanism.

Do not start with a topic.

Do not start with an outline.

Start with the lens.

Examples:

- Buildings have a memory problem.
- AI has a food supply problem.
- People don't share solutions. They share stories.
- Consumers don't have an intelligence problem. They have a routing problem.

The entire article exists to earn that sentence.

---

## Inputs to define

- Lens
- Supporting observations
- Tension
- Evidence
- Target audience
- Desired length
- Writing style

---

## Minimal workflow

### 1. Start with the lens

Identify the sentence the article exists to install.

Example:

```text
Consumers don't have an intelligence problem.

They have a routing problem.
```

Everything else supports this.

---

### 2. Find the tension

Ask:

```text
What contradiction makes this lens necessary?
```

Template:

```text
People expect X.

Yet Y happens.
```

Example:

```text
People expect smarter models to create more value.

Yet most users are performing the same tasks they performed years ago.
```

This becomes Act 1.

---

### 3. Gather observations

Collect evidence.

Examples:

- Writing emails
- Summarising documents
- Tool selection
- Customer support queries
- Repeated workflows

The observations must support the tension.

Do not introduce evidence that does not help earn the lens.

---

### 4. Build the investigation

Ask:

```text
Why does this contradiction exist?
```

Explore.

Do not conclude.

Do not deliver the lens yet.

Good investigation creates curiosity.

Bad investigation explains everything immediately.

---

### 5. Deliver the lens

Only after tension and evidence have accumulated.

The lens should feel inevitable.

The reader should think:

```text
Of course.

That's exactly what's happening.
```

Not:

```text
That's an interesting opinion.
```

---

## Article Structure

### Act 1 — Tension

Start with the contradiction.

Template:

```text
Everyone believes X.

Yet Y keeps happening.

Something doesn't fit.
```

Goal:

Create curiosity.

Reader reaction:

```text
That's strange.
```

---

### Act 2 — Investigation

Explore the contradiction.

Use:

- Observations
- Examples
- Stories
- Analogies
- Counterarguments

Ask:

```text
What is actually happening here?
```

Goal:

Increase understanding.

Reader reaction:

```text
Now I see the pattern.
```

---

### Act 3 — Lens

Deliver the reframe.

Template:

```text
Perhaps the real issue isn't X.

It's Y.
```

Goal:

Install the lens.

Reader reaction:

```text
I hadn't thought about it that way.
```

## Heuristics

### Good Act 1

Contains tension.

Example:

```text
AI keeps getting smarter.

My work doesn't.
```

---

### Good Act 2

Contains evidence.

Example:

```text
GPT-3 could write my email.

GPT-4 could write my email.

Claude could write my email.

The task hasn't changed.
```

---

### Good Act 3

Contains a category shift.

Example:

```text
Consumers don't have an intelligence problem.

They have a routing problem.
```

---

## Anti-Patterns

### Delivering the lens too early

Bad:

```text
Consumers have a routing problem.

Here's why...
```

The article has nothing left to discover.

---

### Endless investigation

Bad:

```text
Observation.
Observation.
Observation.
Observation.
```

No payoff.

---

### Advice instead of lens

Bad:

```text
Companies should use smaller models.
```

Advice.

Not a lens.

---

### Conclusion instead of lens

Bad:

```text
New models are often unnecessary.
```

Conclusion.

Not a lens.

---

## Golden Rule

The reader should move through three states:

```text
That's strange.
        ↓
Now I understand.
        ↓
I never thought about it that way.
```

If those three transitions occur, the lens has been successfully installed.
