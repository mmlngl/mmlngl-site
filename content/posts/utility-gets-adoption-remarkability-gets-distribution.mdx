This week, big AI released another "frontier" model.
Can you feel the hype?

Every AI company wants me to believe I need their newest and best model.
"Powerful". "Smarter". "More capable".

Yet, the tasks I ask it to do haven't changed:
Fix a bug.
Summarise a doc.
Answer a question.

Bugs haven't gotten more complex.
Docs haven't gotten longer.
My questions haven't gotten deeper.

In 2020, GPT-3 could write an email to my boss.
In 2023, GPT-4 could write an email to my boss.
In 2024, Gemini could write an email to my boss.
In 2025, Claude could write an email to my boss.

Whatever frontier launched this week, whatever model launches next month, I'll can use it to bug my boss.

Yet, every launch is sold to me like my life is about to change.

It feels like being pushed to upgrade without a change in usefulness.

\*

In 2025, [METR showed that each Frontier model is better at completing complex tasks](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/). They assumed more complex tasks take longer and compared completion % to task duration.
Result showed that new models are more successful at longer tasks.

But what if the task duration is the same?
What if the complexity doesn't change?
I'm not asking it to refactor a codebase.
I'm asking it to summarise a document.

Openclaw became popular over the last year.
It's an ecosystem and community focused on give AI superpowers to your life.

It's popularity caused an explosion of YT videos describing how to set it up. There's a step in that process when you pick the AI brain you want it to use.

In one video I watched, the guy suggested you set up multiple models. One for small, simple tasks. One more powerful for complex tasks.

He went on: "Use the powerful model for tool selection".

"Tool selection"? That's one of the easier tasks.
The distinction between "check my calendar" and "send an email" is clear. Must we use a powerful model for that?

\*

It made me wonder whether we’re confusing capability with usefulness.

We're not being lied to. Newer models _are_ more intelligent.

Perhaps we don't have an intelligence problem.
We have a model-picking problem.

There's no challenge in finding the smartest model.
It's knowing much intelligence the task actually requires.

The AI industry is obsessed with building faster F1 cars.
Most of us are driving to Tesco.

The next true breakthrough might not be a smarter model.
It might be a system that quietly picks the smallest sufficient one.
